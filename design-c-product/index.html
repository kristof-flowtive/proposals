01Understanding

What you're solving for

Your digital product funnel needs a steady stream of short-form videos, and you don't want to be on camera or in an editor. As a non-technical business owner you want a turnkey system: you enter a core topic or angle, and a finished video shows up in a Google Drive folder labelled by date.

The step most bids skip is assembly. Make.com moves data between apps; it cannot cut video. Pairing the voiceover with visuals needs a cloud render service driven by a template, with captions timed to the voice. That is where most of this build's effort goes, and it is designed in from day one below.

You asked for"High-retention video script under 50 seconds using proven hook frameworks"

A Hook frameworks table in Airtable (myth vs fact, mistake callout, question, number list, pattern interrupt) that you pick from or leave on auto. The script comes back as structured beats with a word cap, and the 50 seconds is checked again on the finished voiceover.

You asked for"The approved script"

Scripts land in Airtable as "Needs approval". Nothing goes to ElevenLabs until you change the status to Approved, so you never pay for a voiceover you'd have rejected. Edit the text in the cell first if you want; the edit is what gets voiced.

You asked for"Relevant cinematic visuals" from stock video APIs or Runway

Each script beat carries its own visual search terms. Make pulls vertical clips per beat from a stock API, never reuses a clip within 30 days, and can send just the hook shot to Runway when you want something no stock library has.

You asked for"Organized folders in Google Drive, labeled by date"

One folder per day (2026-11-24), one file set per video: the MP4, the script and the voiceover. The Drive link is written back to the Airtable row so you can find any video from the dashboard.

Not in your brief, but your niche needs it

Scripts that stay on the right side of credit repair advertising rules

Credit repair claims draw US regulator and platform attention (the Credit Repair Organizations Act and the FTC on misleading claims). The script prompt forbids guaranteed score increases, specific point or timeline promises and "remove accurate negative items" language, and a banned-claims list you control in Airtable is checked before a script reaches you. A guardrail, not legal advice, but it means a fast hook never turns into a claim you'd have to take down.

04Reliability

Built so no video fails silently or ships wrong

01

You approve before money is spent

Script generation costs pennies. Voice and rendering are the paid steps, and neither runs until the row says Approved.

02

50 seconds, checked twice

A word cap in the prompt, then the real length from the ElevenLabs file. A voice that reads slower than expected can't push a video past your limit unnoticed.

03

Compliance guardrail on every script

No guaranteed results, no point or day promises, no "erase accurate items". The prompt forbids them, the check flags them, and you see the flag before approving.

04

No stale or repeated footage

Portrait clips only, a 30-day clip log against repeats, and a fallback to a Drive folder of backup b-roll you choose, so a weak search never leaves a black frame.

05

Fails to you, never to silence

If ElevenLabs, the stock API or the renderer errors, the row goes to Error with a plain-English reason and you get an email. Fix it, set the status back, and it resumes from that step.

06

Your accounts, your keys, a cost cap

Airtable, Make, ElevenLabs, the AI key, the renderer and Drive are all under your logins. A daily render cap in Settings stops a runaway batch.

08Before kickoff

Three things I'll confirm on our first call

Q1

Stock library, Runway, or InVideo?

I'd build on a stock API plus a render service, because both are driven cleanly from Make. InVideo is built around its own editor, so if you have a reason to use it I'll check what its API allows before committing. Default: Pexels, with Runway available for hook shots.

Q2

Which voice, and which look?

Pick an ElevenLabs voice (or let me shortlist three) and send your brand colours and font. Default: one voice, bold word-by-word captions in the lower third, 1080×1920.

Q3

What's the CTA, and what can your product claim?

The end card and the banned-claims list both depend on it. Default: one CTA line in Settings, and a banned list seeded with guarantees, point and day promises, and deleting accurate items. Posting to TikTok or Instagram isn't in scope; Drive is the finish line, as briefed.
