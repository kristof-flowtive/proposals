01Understanding

What you're solving for

Your LinkedIn activity, website, contact management and email marketing are not currently integrated. You want a more systematic way of attracting prospective clients, capturing their interest, keeping them engaged and converting the relevant ones into consultancy assignments, with "good enough" tools that suit a new firm's budget.

The catch most journeys miss: LinkedIn is where your attention goes, but LinkedIn doesn't hand over contact details. Followers, people who react to posts and LinkedIn newsletter subscribers can't be exported to a CRM. So the journey's central job is to give people a reason to move from LinkedIn to something you own, and everything else is built around that hand-off.

You asked for"Design the customer journey"

Five plain stages: Discover (LinkedIn, referrals, events), Subscribe (newsletter or a useful resource on your site), Engage (opens, replies, event sign-ups), Conversation, Opportunity. Each stage has one owner action and one thing the system does for you.

You asked for"whether this should sit within our existing website infrastructure or on a separate platform"

A separate platform. Contacts held inside the website are tied to that build, and the next redesign puts your list at risk. My recommendation is HubSpot: the free CRM to start, Starter when you want branded newsletters and simple automation. Your website keeps its job, with embedded forms feeding the CRM.

You asked for"capture contacts through LinkedIn, the website, newsletter registrations and other relevant channels, with appropriate consent"

Website and newsletter forms with consent wording, LinkedIn posts pointing to an owned sign-up, LinkedIn lead forms synced if you run ads, and a quick-add route for people you meet. Every contact carries its source and its consent basis.

You asked for"an efficient process for managing newsletter subscribers, distributing communications and supporting follow-up"

A welcome email for new subscribers, a newsletter template, and follow-up tasks when someone engages. AI drafts the routine writing (the monthly newsletter from your recent posts, follow-up emails) and you approve before anything is sent.

Not in your brief, but it decides who you can email

UK consent rules split your B2B contacts in two

Under PECR, people at limited companies can be sent relevant marketing email under legitimate interests with a clear opt-out, but sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals and need consent first. A consultancy selling to both needs a lawful-basis field on every contact from day one, so your newsletter only goes to people it legally can. That's built into the forms and the import, not left as a policy document.

04Data and safeguards

Built so a contact is never lost, duplicated or emailed without a basis

01

Your existing contacts

Exported from wherever they live now (LinkedIn connections export, inboxes, spreadsheets), cleaned, deduplicated and imported with a lawful basis assigned, not guessed.

02

The same person signs up twice

Matched on email, so a second form fill updates the record and adds the new source instead of creating a duplicate.

03

An automation fails

The failure is logged and emailed to you with the contact it affected. Nothing fails silently.

04

AI writes something wrong

Every AI draft is a draft. Follow-ups and newsletters wait for your approval; the AI note is clearly marked as AI and uses public information only.

05

Someone unsubscribes or objects

Opt-outs are honoured across every list and automation at once, and kept on record so they are never re-imported.

06

You outgrow it

Everything is in your own accounts. Moving from the free tier to Starter, or adding a colleague, changes nothing about the setup.
