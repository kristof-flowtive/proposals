01Understanding

What you're solving for

Your LinkedIn activity, website, contact management and email marketing are not currently integrated. You want a simple, effective and scalable way of attracting prospective clients, capturing their interest, keeping them engaged and converting the relevant ones into consultancy assignments, run by a small team with limited admin capacity.

The catch most journeys miss: LinkedIn is where your attention goes, but LinkedIn doesn't hand over contact details. Followers, people who react to posts and LinkedIn newsletter subscribers can't be exported to a CRM. So the journey's central job is to give people a reason to move from LinkedIn to something you own, and the automation is kept to what reduces manual work without compromising the quality of personal engagement.

Your Terms of Reference"… engage with our content, become identifiable contacts and progress towards potential business opportunities"

Five plain stages: Discover (LinkedIn, referrals, events), Subscribe (newsletter or a briefing on your site), Engage, Conversation, Opportunity. Each has one action for you and one thing the system does, drawn as the visual journey map you asked for.

Your Terms of Reference"Assess whether our CRM should be integrated into the website or hosted on a separate platform"

My starting view is a separate platform: contacts held inside a website are tied to that build, and the next redesign puts your list at risk. In phase 2 you get the concise comparison you asked for (HubSpot, Brevo, and keeping it in the website with your current newsletter tool) with costs and a clear recommendation. I expect it to be HubSpot.

Your Terms of Reference"Define how contacts should be categorised, including newsletter subscribers, prospective clients, existing clients and business development opportunities"

Those four become one Contact type field, plus source and lawful basis. Existing clients get the newsletter but are never put into prospecting follow-ups. Opportunities move into a simple deal pipeline.

Your Terms of Reference"LinkedIn lead capture mechanisms, where supported by the platform"

What LinkedIn supports: posts linking to an owned sign-up, Lead Gen Forms synced to the CRM if you run ads, and a quick-add route for people you connect with or meet. What it doesn't support (exporting followers or post engagers) is said plainly up front, so nobody expects it.

Your Terms of Reference"A simple mechanism for identifying contacts requiring personal business development follow-up"

A contact who replies, downloads a briefing or opens several issues in a row is flagged and gets a task for the right person, with a suggested follow-up email drafted by AI. You edit or skip it; nothing personal goes out unseen.

Your Terms of Reference"prioritise existing integrations and low-code/no-code solutions over bespoke software development"

No custom code. HubSpot's own forms, website plugin and LinkedIn connection do the heavy lifting; Make is used only where a native connection doesn't exist, for example the AI newsletter draft. Everything stays in your accounts.

Not in your brief, but it decides who you can email

UK consent rules split your B2B contacts in two

Under PECR, people at limited companies can be sent relevant marketing email under legitimate interests with a clear opt-out, but sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals and need consent first. In responsible sourcing and impact work, plenty of contacts are independent advisers. A lawful-basis field on every contact from day one, set by the forms and the import, keeps your newsletter going only to people it legally can.

04Data and safeguards

Built so a contact is never lost, duplicated or emailed without a basis

01

Your existing contacts

Exported from wherever they live now (LinkedIn connections export, inboxes, spreadsheets, your current newsletter tool), cleaned, deduplicated and imported with a contact type and lawful basis assigned, not guessed.

02

The same person signs up twice

Matched on email, so a second form fill updates the record and adds the new source instead of creating a duplicate.

03

An automation fails

The failure is logged and emailed to you with the contact it affected. Nothing fails silently.

04

AI writes something wrong

Every AI draft is a draft. Newsletters and follow-ups wait for your approval, so the quality of personal engagement stays in your hands.

05

Someone unsubscribes or objects

Opt-outs and preference changes are honoured across every list and automation at once, and kept on record so they are never re-imported.

06

You grow

Everything is in your own accounts. Moving from a free tier to a paid one, or adding a colleague, changes nothing about the setup.

08Before kickoff

Three things I'll confirm on our first call

Q1

Which newsletter tool and website platform do you use today?

It decides whether the newsletter moves into the CRM or stays where it is and syncs. Default: move it into the CRM if the comparison shows it's cheaper and simpler.

Q2

Who on your team will run the system day to day?

Training and the operating instructions are written for that person. Default: one owner, trained in a recorded session, with a second person shown the basics.

Q3

Do you run, or plan to run, LinkedIn ads?

Lead Gen Forms are the only way LinkedIn passes contact details directly. Default: set up the connection ready to use, with organic posts pointing to your site in the meantime.
