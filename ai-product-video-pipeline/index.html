01 — Understanding

What you're solving for

Ninety household appliances need a video and an image set each, inside two months, and the appliance has to be recognisably yours. Anyone can make an AI clip of "a kettle". The job is making a clip of your kettle, with its handle, its dial, its badge, ninety times over, without the output drifting. That is a workflow problem before it is a creative one, which is why your posting asks for a workflow builder.

Requirement

"One 10 second AI video and a set of 4 to 6 lifestyle images" per product

Ninety videos and roughly 450 images. The pipeline generates several candidates per product and keeps the ones that pass accuracy, so the numbers are a batch, not ninety separate briefs.

Requirement

"Deliberate camera angles and movement, then finish it in post editing"

A shot list per product category (slow orbit for a stand mixer, push-in on the control panel for an oven) drives camera prompts. Post is a reusable template: colour, grade, your end card, sound bed, export presets per channel.

Requirement

"Keep every product detail accurate, including shape, colour, controls and logos"

The part most AI video gets wrong, so the pipeline is built around it: generation conditioned on your reference photos, a per-product checklist against the spec sheet, and a reject-and-regenerate loop before anything reaches post.

Bonus you asked for

"Technical background in coding or AI automation using n8n / ComfyUI"

That is the build: n8n orchestrates the batch from your product sheet, ComfyUI graphs produce the image sets, Python handles files and checks, and the whole thing is handed over so your team can run the next ninety.

03 — Your controls

Everything you'd want to change is a cell in one sheet

The pipeline reads the sheet at the start of every product, so a change to the shot list or the brand rules takes effect on the next run. No need to open n8n or ComfyUI.

  • ▸Products tab — one row per SKU: status, references, approved take, delivery.
  • ▸Shot list tab — scenes and camera moves per category, in plain English.
  • ▸Brand rules tab — what must never appear, logo treatment, colour references.
  • ▸Approvals tab — candidate thumbnails with approve / reject and a reason box.
  • ▸Costs tab — generation spend per product, so there are no surprises at the end.

04 — Accuracy

Built so the appliance on screen is your appliance

01

References anchor every frame

Image generation is conditioned on your product photos, not described from text alone. The scene changes; the product does not.

02

Video starts from a correct still

Image-to-video from an approved hero frame keeps shape and colour through the camera move far better than text-to-video does.

03

Checklist per product

Shape, colour, control layout, logo placement, checked against the spec sheet before a candidate reaches your board. Fails are regenerated, not argued over.

04

Logos handled in post when needed

Where a model still softens a badge, the clean logo is tracked and composited in the edit. The honest answer to a known weakness.

05

Cost cap per product

A product that keeps failing stops after a set number of attempts and flags for a human look, so one awkward SKU cannot burn the batch budget.

06

Yours, in your accounts

n8n workflows, ComfyUI graphs, model API keys and the sheet all live under your logins. After handover your team can run product 91.

07 — The same pattern, elsewhere

Two worked examples of this architecture

Illustrative scenarios, not client case studies. They show how the same building blocks, a sheet-driven batch, reference-conditioned generation, an accuracy gate and a human approval board, bend to other catalogues.

Worked example · Furniture retailer

Two hundred sofas, each shown in four rooms it was never photographed in

The merchandising team has one cut-out photo per sofa and wants lifestyle imagery for the website and paid social. The pipeline conditions on the cut-out, places each sofa in four room scenes from the shot list, checks fabric colour and leg shape against the product data, and sends candidates to a board where a merchandiser approves one per room. Rejected scenes carry the note back into the next attempt.

Short product clips for marketplace listings, regenerated whenever the range changes

08 — Before kickoff

Three things I'll confirm on our first call

Q1
What reference material exists per product?
Three to six clean photos per appliance is the ideal input. If some products only have one, the pilot will include one of those so we see the difference.
Q2
Where will the videos run?
Marketplace listings, paid social and your own site each want different aspect ratios, lengths and end cards. I'll assume 16:9 and 9:16 with a brand end card unless you say otherwise.
Q3
Who approves, and how fast?
Eight weeks for ninety products needs approvals turned around within a day or two. One named approver on your side keeps the batch moving.
