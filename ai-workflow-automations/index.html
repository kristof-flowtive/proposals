01 — Understanding

What you're solving for

Manual work is eating hours you need for growth: leads waiting for a reply, emails and PDFs retyped into a sheet, reports pulled together by hand. You want to take each process "from idea to a working, tested system", connected to the tools you already use, and then stop thinking about it.

Your brief lists five kinds of use case against one budget. The honest way to make that work is to pick the right ones first, in writing, and then build them properly. That is how this plan is shaped.

REQUIREMENT
"Review my current processes and recommend"

Week one produces an automation map: each candidate process with hours saved per week, the apps it touches, whether AI is needed, running cost and risk. You choose what gets built.

REQUIREMENT
AI "where it genuinely helps"

Claude handles the judgement steps: qualifying a lead, drafting a reply, pulling fields out of a PDF. Routing, dates and lookups stay as plain logic, which is cheaper and never improvises.

REQUIREMENT
"Errors trigger a clear alert or log entry"

Every workflow shares one error path: a plain-English row in an Errors tab, a Slack or Gmail alert, and the item parked for a person. Nothing fails quietly.

REQUIREMENT
"Test every workflow with real data"

Each workflow runs on your real leads, emails and files before handover, and your definition of done is the sign-off checklist, line by line.

REQUIREMENT
"All accounts, credentials, and workflows are handed over to me" · "so I can maintain it"

Built in your Make (or n8n), Anthropic and app accounts from day one, so there is nothing to transfer at the end. One written doc per workflow plus a recorded walkthrough, and every setting you might change lives in a Google Sheet rather than inside the scenario.

04 — Reliability

Built so a failure is caught the same day, not next month

01

Fails to a person, never to silence

If Claude, the CRM or Gmail errors, an error handler on that step writes the Errors row, sends the alert and parks the item in Review. The lead is still there on Monday.

02

No duplicates when a form fires twice

Each item's source ID goes into a Make data store on first sight. A double-submit or webhook retry is logged and skipped, so nobody gets two follow-up emails.

03

AI output checked before it's trusted

Claude answers in a fixed JSON shape. A missing field or a score out of range never reaches your CRM; it gets one retry, then goes to Review.

04

Human review where your brand is on the line

Customer replies and generated content start in draft mode. You read a week of drafts, then flip one cell to live. Generated reports keep a review step for good, as your brief asks.

05

Costs that can't run away

A daily cap on AI calls sits in the sheet. If a loop or a spam burst hits it, workflows switch to log-and-park and you get one alert, not a bill.

06

Yours from day one

Scenarios, API keys and connections are created under your logins. Handover is a checklist, not a migration. If we part ways, nothing stops working.

07 — The same pattern, elsewhere

Two worked examples of this architecture

Illustrative scenarios, not client case studies. They show how the same spine, a trigger, Make in the middle, Claude on the judgement step, an owner-editable sheet and a shared error path, bends to different businesses.

WORKED EXAMPLE · RECRUITMENT AGENCY

CVs from Gmail into the CRM, scored against the live job

Applications arrive as Gmail attachments. Make picks up each email, Claude extracts name, contact details, notice period, salary and skills from the CV into a fixed schema, then scores the candidate against the job spec held in the sheet. Strong matches create a CRM record and ping the consultant in Slack; weak ones are filed with a reason. A CV that won't parse goes to Review with the original attached, so nobody is lost.

The owner approves drafts in Slack for the first week, then switches to live. Every Monday a second scenario counts the week's enquiries by source and outcome, Claude writes three lines on what changed, and it lands in Slack after a quick review.

08 — Before kickoff

Four things I'll confirm on our first call

Q1
Which task eats the most hours each week right now?

That becomes workflow 1. If you're unsure, I'll assume lead capture, qualification and follow-up, because a slow reply to a lead costs money every day it stays manual.

Q2
Are you already paying for Make, n8n or Zapier, and do you want self-hosting?

Default is Make in your account: visual, quick to hand over, and cheaper than Zapier at multi-step volumes. n8n if you want self-hosting or heavier custom code. Zapier only if your team already lives in it.

Q3
Which CRM, and does your plan include API access?

Some entry-level CRM plans lock the API. If yours does, I'll tell you before kickoff, and the fallback is a Google Sheet as the lead record until you upgrade.

Q4
Is WhatsApp in scope, and through which provider?

Automated WhatsApp needs the Business API through a provider such as respond.io or Twilio, not the phone app. If you don't have one yet, I'll keep WhatsApp out of the first three and leave it on the map as the next build.
