01 — Understanding

What you're solving for

Your ads already make the phone buzz. Then the responsibility is passed to the business owner or salesperson, and that is where it leaks: leads aren't responded to quickly enough, enquiries aren't properly qualified, and nobody follows up. You want to sell a fully managed fix to Singapore service businesses, where WhatsApp is one of the primary channels, without becoming the technical support desk yourself.

Your most important requirement, answered straight

"Proven results" and "how much of the improvement can reasonably be attributed to the system"

I don't have a published client case study with before-and-after numbers to show you yet, and I won't dress one up. What I do have is a past WhatsApp AI build I can walk you through, a design built specifically for this kind of service business, and a pilot that measures itself: a baseline from your last 60 days of enquiries, the same metrics after go-live, and a written note on what the system can and can't take credit for. That report is the case study you take to your first client. How it's measured.

Requirement

"Respond instantly to new enquiries through WhatsApp"

Every form, Meta lead ad and WhatsApp message gets a reply in under a minute, 24/7, answering services, pricing, process and availability from that client's own FAQ, never from guesswork.

Requirement

"I don't want AI to completely replace human salespeople"

The AI qualifies and books; the consultation, relationship-building and closing stay with the owner or sales team. Handover rules (asks for a person, complaint, out-of-scope, high-value job) live in each client's sheet.

Requirement

"Without requiring a complete rebuild for every new client"

One GHL snapshot, one Claude relay, one config sheet row per client. A new aesthetic clinic or waterproofing contractor is a clone plus a filled-in sheet, answering leads in draft mode within 3–5 working days.

Requirement

"I'm not a technical person"

I own the backend: deployment, monitoring, fixes and improvements. You see results in a dashboard and a weekly summary, and never have to open Make, n8n or a WhatsApp template manager.

03 — Per-client template

The difference between a renovation company and an aesthetic clinic is content, not plumbing.   • ▸Qualifying tab — the questions per industry (unit type for renovation, treatment area for clinics) and what counts as qualified.
  • ▸Log and Errors tabs — everything the AI did and anything that failed, in plain language, visible without asking me.

04 — WhatsApp in Singapore

The platform rules are designed in, not discovered mid-build

Most "AI WhatsApp bots" are demoed inside a conversation the tester started. Reminders, no-show follow-ups and old-lead re-engagement are the opposite case, and they run on different rules.

01

The 24-hour window

After a lead messages, the business can reply freely for 24 hours. Claude handles the live conversation inside that window; nothing outside it is sent as free text.

02

Templates for everything after

Reminders, no-show rebooks, chases and form-lead first touches go out as Meta-approved templates. The snapshot ships with a tested set per industry, submitted on day one of each client because approval takes time.

03

One number per client business

Each client gets their own WhatsApp Business number under their own Meta business, in their display name. Leads see the clinic, not your agency, and the number stays theirs.

04

Meta's fees are per template message

Replies inside the window cost nothing from Meta; templates are charged per message by category at Singapore rates. Follow-up cadences are set with that in mind, and each client's monthly fee estimate is in the pilot report.

05

PDPA consent before re-engagement

"Re-engage old leads" is marketing under the PDPA and the Do Not Call rules. New forms capture consent explicitly; old lists are only messaged where consent is on record, which we check per client before anything is sent.

06

Existing WhatsApp numbers

Many clients already use the WhatsApp Business app on their main number. Whether to move that number onto the API, use coexistence, or add a new number is a per-client call I'll make with you at onboarding.

05 — Proof

The pilot measures itself, so it becomes your case study

You asked what was achieved, how it was measured and how much is attributable. Those three questions are the spec for the pilot report, and the same measurement is built into every client you deploy afterwards.

Every rate is reported with its count ("14 of 52"), never as a bare percentage.

What's cleanly attributable

Response time and recovered leads. The system either replied in 40 seconds or it didn't, and a rebooked no-show is tagged with the message that recovered it.

What needs caveats

Booking and show rates also move with ad spend, offer, season and lead volume. The report records spend and campaigns over both periods, and flags any change that muddies the comparison.

If the numbers are small

Your agency's own lead volume may be modest. If so, the report says so, and your first client (with the same measurement on) becomes the stronger case study. An optional holdout, some leads handled the old way, gives the cleanest read if you want it.

06 — Reliability

Built so no enquiry is dropped silently, in any client account

01

Fails to the client's team, never to silence

If Claude, Make or the sheet errors, the lead is still assigned in GHL with a note that the AI couldn't answer, and the Errors tab and my alert fire.

02

No invented prices or promises

Claude only quotes what the Knowledge tab says ("from $X, final quote at the site visit"). A question it can't answer from the sheet is a handover, not a guess.

03

Draft mode for every new client

New clients start in draft: the AI writes, the team approves inside GHL. Switching to live is one cell in the sheet, once a week of drafts reads right.

04

Caps against loops and runaway cost

A per-conversation lock stops double replies, and a daily reply cap per client sends everything to handover-only if something unusual starts happening.

05

Singapore hours, UK support

Singapore is 8 hours ahead of me, so the system is built to run unattended through your day. My end-of-day update lands in your morning, and 4–6pm SGT is our overlap for calls.

06

In your accounts, not mine

GHL agency, Make, Anthropic key and each client's Meta business sit under your or the client's ownership. If we part ways, nothing stops working.
