01 — Understanding

What you're solving for

Leads arrive from your website form, Facebook lead ads and Angi, and three office staff call them back when they get a chance. On a busy day that is 3-4 hours later, and by then half of them have booked with someone else. A roofing lead who filled in a form at 9pm is comparing three companies; the one that texts back first usually gets the estimate.

You already have GoHighLevel. You want the AI to do the texting and the booking, your office to keep the final say on any lead, and nothing for you to manage afterwards.

REQUIREMENT
A text within 2 minutes, 7 days a week, even at night

The "got your request, when's a good time for an estimate?" text is sent by a GoHighLevel workflow the moment the contact is created. It does not wait for the AI, so it goes out in seconds even if everything else is down.

REQUIREMENT
AI handles the back-and-forth and books the estimate

Claude reads the thread, asks what it needs (address, roof issue, best times), checks your GoHighLevel calendar for open slots and books the appointment. The lead gets the normal GHL confirmation and reminders.

REQUIREMENT
Hand it to a human in your office with a notification

If the lead asks something the AI can't answer, gets annoyed, or asks for a person, the contact is tagged needs-human, assigned to your office and a GHL notification goes out. The AI stops on that lead.

REQUIREMENT
Office staff can pause the AI for a specific lead

One tag, ai-paused, added from the GHL inbox or mobile app. The AI checks for it before every reply. Remove the tag and it picks the thread back up.

REQUIREMENT
A daily summary to the owner of leads in, booked, lost

Every morning at a time you choose, a short email (and a text if you want it): leads in by source, estimates booked, leads waiting on a human, leads lost and why.

REQUIREMENT
Nothing to manage afterwards

Written documentation, a handover video of the build, and a five-minute video for the office on pausing, taking over and changing the AI's wording. Everything you might change lives in one Google Sheet.

04 — Reliability

Built so no lead waits 3-4 hours again, and none is dropped silently

01

The 2-minute text never waits for the AI

It is a plain GoHighLevel workflow step, sent from your number the moment the contact exists. If Make or Claude is ever down, the lead still hears from you and your office gets a notification to pick it up.

02

Fails to a human, never to silence

Any error, any question the instructions don't cover, any sign of frustration, or a lead asking for a person: tag needs-human, assign to your office, notify. The lead gets a short "someone from the office will text you shortly" so they are not left hanging.

03

Pause means pause

The ai-paused tag is checked before every reply, not once at the start. Before each reply Make also reads the thread from GHL; if the last outbound text came from a person, the AI treats the lead as taken over and stays quiet.

04

One reply per burst, no double-booking

A per-contact lock in Make's data store means two texts seconds apart can't trigger two Claude calls or two appointments. Availability is re-checked at the moment of booking, not when it was first offered.

05

2am handled, Central time, within the rules

A lead who texts at 2am gets answered at 2am; they started the conversation. The AI never starts one in quiet hours: nudges to a silent lead wait until 8am America/Chicago. A turn cap per lead stops any loop, and every text carries the opt-out wording US carriers expect.

06

Yours, in your accounts

GHL workflows, Make scenarios, the Anthropic key and the Google Sheet all live under your logins. The documentation and handover video mean any competent GHL person could maintain it. If we part ways, nothing stops working.

07 — The same pattern, elsewhere

Two worked examples of this architecture

Illustrative scenarios, not client case studies. They show how the same building blocks, a CRM doing the sending, Make in the middle, Claude with booking tools, and an owner-editable sheet, bend to other home-service businesses.

WORKED EXAMPLE · HVAC COMPANY

Facebook leads texted in a minute, service calls round-robined across techs

An HVAC company runs Facebook lead ads all summer and loses leads to whoever answers first. The lead ad form drops the contact into GHL, the first text goes out in under a minute, and Claude asks whether it is a repair or a replacement, confirms the ZIP is in the service area, and offers the next two slots from a round-robin calendar across four technicians. Emergencies ("no AC, 100 degrees, kids at home") are handed straight to the dispatcher with a notification instead of being booked days out. The owner edits the service area and the emergency phrases in the sheet.

08 — Before kickoff

Three things I'll confirm on our first call

Q1
Have you sent texts from GoHighLevel before?

US carriers require A2P 10DLC registration before business texting works reliably. If you are already texting from GHL, nothing to do. If not, I file it on day 1, because approval is the one thing in this build I can't speed up, and the 2-minute text goes live the day it clears.

Q2
Whose calendar does an estimate land on, and how do you split the territory?

Forty crews do the roofing; usually a few estimators do the visits. Default: one Estimates calendar in GHL, round-robin across your estimators, 30-minute travel buffers, and the lead's ZIP deciding who is eligible. If you already have calendars set up, I use those.

Q3
How do Angi leads reach you today, and what counts as "lost"?

Default: Angi's lead emails are forwarded to an inbox Make watches and parsed into a contact; if your Angi account can push leads by webhook I'll use that instead. For the summary, "lost" defaults to said no, booked elsewhere, or silent after the third nudge. You can change the nudge timings in the sheet.
