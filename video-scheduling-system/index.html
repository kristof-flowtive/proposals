01Understanding

What you're solving for

You're about to publish tons of finished videos: client stories, culture content and AI thought-leadership pieces from your founder. Writing four sets of copy per video and posting each one by hand would eat a person's week. You want a system where AI does the heavy lifting, your team runs it every week, and you own all of it.

The detail that decides the quality: AI can't write a good caption for a client story from a file name. So every video is transcribed first, and Claude writes from what is actually said, the kind of video it is, and your brand voice. And one video rarely fits all four channels the same way. A landscape client story is a YouTube video and a LinkedIn post; a vertical cut is a Reel. The system reads each video's shape and proposes the right channels, and your team can change them before approving.

You asked for"Pull videos from a shared folder"

One shared Google Drive folder with a subfolder per content type: Client stories, Culture, Founder. Your editors drop finished files in; the folder they choose tells the AI what kind of video it is. Nothing else to fill in.

You asked for"Generate captions, titles, descriptions, and hashtags in our brand voice"

A Brand voice table holds your tone, words to use and avoid, approved hashtags and a few example posts you like. Claude writes one version per channel: a LinkedIn post, a YouTube title and description, a Facebook post, an Instagram caption with hashtags.

You asked for"A simple content calendar and approval step before publishing"

An Airtable calendar with a review screen for your team: watch the video, edit any line of copy, pick the slot, click Approve. Nothing posts without that click.

You asked for"A written SOP, a recorded walkthrough, a live training session, and full ownership of all accounts, workflows, and logins"

Handover is a milestone of its own, not a last-day extra. Everything is built in accounts you own from day one, and the SOP covers the weekly routine and the two things that need a human: reconnecting a channel and fixing a failed post.

Not in your brief, but it shapes the schedule

Each platform has its own limits on automated posting

On a default Google API quota, YouTube allows roughly six uploads a day per project, so a big backlog is queued across days rather than pushed at once. LinkedIn and Meta connections expire or need re-approval from time to time. The calendar plans around the quota, and an expired connection raises an alert with a two-minute fix in the SOP instead of failing quietly.

04Reliability

Built so no post fails, or doubles, without you knowing

01

A post fails

The channel is marked Failed with the platform's reason, an alert goes out, and Retry is one click once it's fixed. The other channels for that video carry on.

02

A connection expires

Social platforms ask for reconnection from time to time. The alert names the channel, and the SOP shows the two-minute reconnect, done by your team in your account.

03

Nothing posts twice

Once a channel is marked Posted it is locked. A video moved or re-uploaded to the folder is matched by file ID and not drafted again.

04

A video doesn't fit a channel

A landscape-only video is held for Reels rather than cropped badly, and your team can drop the channel or add a vertical cut.

05

The AI gets something wrong

It writes only from the transcript and your Brand voice table, never names a client unless the folder note says it's approved, and every post passes your approval first.

06

You own everything

Drive, Airtable, Make, the AI keys and every channel connection are in your accounts, with logins in your password manager. If we part ways, nothing stops working.

08Before kickoff

Three things I'll confirm on our first call

Q1

Should the founder's videos post to the company page, the founder's own LinkedIn, or both?

Thought leadership usually lands better from a personal profile, but that needs its own connection, signed in by the founder. Default: company page from day one, the founder's profile added once they connect it.

Q2

Do your editors export vertical cuts as well as landscape?

It decides what goes to Instagram as a Reel. Default: landscape goes to YouTube, LinkedIn and Facebook; vertical cuts go to Instagram and Facebook; anything else is held for a decision.

Q3

Roughly how many videos a week, and how large are the files?

Volume sets the posting rhythm and the Make plan; very large masters may need a compressed export for posting. Default: up to 15 videos a week, files under a few hundred MB.
