00 — Understanding

What you're solving for

You want to reduce repetitive customer service work without giving up accurate responses and smooth human handovers. Deposits come first, and today every deposit question runs Customer to CS to Payments Team to External Payment Provider to Payments Team to CS to Customer. Each hop is a wait, and each one is a chance for the customer to be asked the same thing again.

Your brief

"Accurate responses"

The AI never tells a player where their money is from its own reasoning. It reads the transaction record, picks the matching case, and fills an approved reply. If it can't find the transaction or the record is ambiguous, it hands over.

Your brief

"Smooth human handovers"

Every handover arrives with a one-line summary, the deposit it is about (amount, method, time, provider reference, current status) and what the player has already been told. The agent starts at step three, not step one.

Your brief

"Multiple markets, brands and languages"

One market on LiveChat first, built so a second brand or language is a new row of settings and translated templates, not a rebuild. LINE plugs into the same middle layer later.

Your brief

"Live deployment requires testing and our approval"

Nothing replies to a player until you sign it off. The test starts in shadow mode, where the AI only suggests replies to agents inside LiveChat, and the numbers from that phase decide whether any bucket goes live.

01 — First steps

Measure where deposit chats actually go before building anything

Your CS head's ranking tells you deposits are the volume. It doesn't yet tell you which deposit cases need Payments and which only need someone who can see the transaction. That split decides the whole design, so it comes first.

STEP 1

Tag 300 recent deposit chats

Export anonymised transcripts from LiveChat for the pilot market. Claude sorts them into cases, an agent spot-checks a sample, and for each case we record: did it go to Payments, did Payments need the provider, how long did it take end to end.

STEP 2

Sit with Payments for one hour

Watch how a payments agent answers a typical escalation. Which screen do they open, which fields do they read, when do they contact the provider? That screen is what the AI needs read access to.

STEP 3

Confirm what can be read by API

Check whether the back office (or the provider's merchant API) exposes deposit status by player ID, and whether LiveChat passes a verified player ID into the chat. If either is missing, that's the first change to make, AI or not.

03 — Tools and information

Few tools, chosen so player data stays with you

The hard requirement isn't the model, it's read access to the transaction record. Everything else is small.

Channel

LiveChat, as you have it

Webhooks for incoming chats, plus a small panel in the agent's view showing the draft and the record behind it. No new tool for your agents to learn. LINE uses the same middle layer later.

Middleware

A small service in your cloud

Player IDs and payment data shouldn't pass through a general automation platform in a regulated business. A small service you host and own keeps it inside your environment and your logging.

Model

Claude API, structured output

Strong at multilingual classification and following fixed templates. Every call returns fixed JSON (case, confidence, draft, summary) that the service checks before anything is shown. Only the fields needed are sent, not the player profile.

Data

Read-only deposit status

From your back office, or from the payment provider's merchant API if the back office lags. By player ID: last few deposits with status, method, amount, timestamps, provider reference and decline code.

Content

Your approved replies per case

Your CS head writes or approves one template per case and the normal processing times per method. The AI fills them; it doesn't invent policy.

Handover

Whatever Payments uses today

Ticket tool, Slack channel or email: the escalation lands where Payments already works, pre-filled. I'd rather fit your process than add a new queue.

04 — Limitations

What could stop this working, written down first

A constraint found in scoping costs a sentence. One found mid-pilot costs a week.

01

No API to the transaction record

The biggest risk. Without read access the AI can only collect details and pre-fill tickets, which still helps, but the Payments hop stays. Assumption to validate in week one.

02

Player not identified in the chat

If chats can start logged out, the bot can't safely look anything up. It then asks the player to log in, or hands over. No lookups by email or name alone.

03

The record is behind reality

If the back office updates late from the provider, "pending" may already be credited. The test measures how often the record was wrong, and the replies set expectations rather than promise times.

04

Licence and compliance rules per market

Some regulators expect disclosure that a player is talking to automation, and responsible gambling rules apply to every reply. Your compliance team approves the templates and the handover rules before anything goes live.

05

Language quality in smaller markets

Drafts from approved templates keep wording safe, but a native-speaking agent should review the translated templates once. Shadow mode shows edit rates per language.

06

The real case mix is different

If tagging shows most deposit chats truly need the provider, the win shifts from answering to a better ticket and an automatic update to the player. The first steps tell us which before money is spent on the build.
