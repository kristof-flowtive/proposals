01 — Understanding

What you're solving for

Your prompt library is written by human industry experts, and today a user has to come to your site to use it. You want it where she already works. She saves her profile once, her business, audience, voice and goals, then types a rough request like "LinkedIn post about my launch, keep it short" and gets back a full prompt built from her profile plus your library.

The code is the smaller half of this job. Three parts of your brief are decided by other companies' rules: how sign-in must work for Claude and ChatGPT to connect, what each directory's reviewers ask for, and where a paid plan is allowed to be sold. Those are designed in below, before a line is written.

Requirement

"A user saves her profile once"

The profile belongs to her account on the engine, not to any one plugin. She signs in from Claude, ChatGPT or the Chrome button with the same account, so a profile saved in one is there in the other two. She can view, change or delete it from any of them.

Requirement

"Using her profile plus our human-written prompt library"

The engine first picks the library prompt that fits her request, then Claude Haiku merges it with her profile and her rough line. Your experts' structure stays intact; Haiku fills it in, it does not replace it. Only the finished prompt is returned. The library itself never leaves your server.

Requirement

"A free plan with usage limits, built so we can switch on paid plans (Stripe) later"

Every account has a plan and a monthly counter, checked in the engine on every request, so the limit is the same in all three places and can't be dodged by switching door. The Stripe webhook and the plan table are built in milestone 1 and left switched off. Turning paid on later is a setting and a price, not a rebuild.

Requirement

"Prepare everything needed for both directory submissions and reviews"

Both directories ask for the same core pack: every tool titled and marked read-only or not, a published privacy policy, a documentation page, a support contact, an icon, and a reviewer test account with a filled-in profile. I prepare the pack once and file both submissions from your accounts.

Not in your brief, but it shapes the paid plan

The upgrade has to happen on your website, not inside ChatGPT

OpenAI's submission rules do not let an app sell digital products or subscriptions, or link straight to a checkout page. So when a free user hits her limit, the plugin tells her so and points to her account page on your site, where Stripe takes over. The engine only reads which plan she is on. Building it this way from day one means switching on paid plans later does not put your ChatGPT listing at risk.

04 — Reliability

Built to protect the library and survive other people's updates

01

Your library is the product, so it stays home

No tool lists or returns library prompts. Requests are rate-limited per account as well as per month, so the plugin can't be used to copy the library one prompt at a time.

02

Limits live in the engine

The count is kept on your server against her account. Reinstalling the extension or switching from ChatGPT to Claude does not reset it.

03

When a chat site changes its layout

ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini redesign their chat boxes without notice. The extension reads where to place the button from a small config on your server, so a fix is a config change, not a new store review. If it can't find the box, the button hides and logs the miss.

04

Review dates are not ours to promise

Anthropic, OpenAI and Google each review on their own clock. So each milestone is "tested and working in your accounts, submission filed". Your testers use the plugins from that day; the public listing follows when each review clears, and I handle reviewer feedback.

05

Profiles are personal data

All three stores ask what you collect and why. The engine stores the profile and a usage count, not her conversations. She can delete her profile herself, and I write up the data facts your privacy policy needs.

06

Your GitHub, your accounts, your keys

Original code committed to your repository as it is written. Hosting, database, Claude API key, Stripe and all three developer accounts are yours. If we part ways, nothing stops working.

08 — Before kickoff

Three things I'll confirm on our first call

Q1
Which server is "our server", and where does the library live today?
The engine needs a host that can run server code around the clock, alongside your website rather than inside it. Default: a small managed host and Postgres database in accounts you own, with your library imported once and then edited in the admin table, or read from your website's CMS if that is where your experts write.
Q2
Inside Claude and ChatGPT, should she see the full prompt, or just the result?
In Chrome the full prompt lands in her chat box. Inside Claude and ChatGPT the plugin hands it to the assistant, which can show it to her first or simply act on it. Default: show the prompt, then answer it, so she sees what your experts' library added.
Q3
Do your users already have accounts on your site?
If they do, the plugins should sign in with that same login rather than create a second one. It depends on what your membership tool supports. Default: a dedicated sign-in service in your account, matched to existing users by email. I'll also confirm which Claude Haiku version you want.
